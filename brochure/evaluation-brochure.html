1 · What it covers

Three jobs, one number, one login.

The question a committee actually asks is not what does it do — it is does this replace things, or is it a tenth login nobody opens. It replaces three.

The outreach

Camps, recalls, price lists, festival greetings, reactivation. Upload a list, send it one patient at a time on a human interval, and stop it mid-run from the same screen. Every recipient carries an outcome and, when it failed, the reason it failed.

The conversation

Enquiries answered out of your own uploaded documents, at your own pace, with follow-ups scheduled days out. Any single chat can be handed back to a person with one switch, and the automation stands down for that person only.

The record

Every photograph, PDF and lab sheet you forward to yourself is read, attached to the right patient, added to a dated timeline, and grouped under a subject index so a whole category can be pulled at once.

2 · How it works

Plain mechanics, and the one detail behind each that only shows up in practice.

It sends the way a person sends.

One message, a pause, the next one. You set the pace before the run starts; you can pause, resume or stop it while it is in flight, and it stops at the message it is on rather than finishing the batch.

The detail: the default gap is 25 seconds and is re-randomised for every message. The randomiser is clamped so the gap can never fall below 30 seconds or 15% of your chosen interval, whichever is larger — a jitter value entered wrongly cannot turn a campaign into a burst. A send that fails waits 10 seconds and continues rather than aborting the run.

The stop flag is checked every second during the wait, so pressing stop takes effect inside the current gap, not at the end of the list.

Every message is drafted before it is sent — and chosen by you.

Sending four hundred people one identical block of text is what gets a number reported. The assistant drafts a pool of rewordings of your message; the run rotates through the pool and substitutes each patient's name and fields.

The detail: the pool is refreshed every 100 contacts on long lists so the back half of a list is not a repeat of the front. If drafting fails, the run falls back to your original message — never to a blank, never to silence.

Nothing is scheduled or sent until a human selects a wording. The schedule screen refuses to proceed with no wording chosen.

The number stays linked through restarts and updates.

The pairing that links your WhatsApp number to the system lives in your database, not in a folder on a server that a deployment wipes. Software updates do not cost you a QR scan or a Monday morning of dead automations.

The detail: each kind of disconnection gets its own retry ladder rather than one blanket delay — around half a second for a routine restart, widening geometrically to a ten-minute ceiling for a hard logout — with up to 3 seconds of random jitter added so a fleet of numbers never reconnects in lockstep.

A pairing that keeps failing is retried twice before the system gives up and asks for a fresh scan, rather than looping on a broken session indefinitely.

A patient who types four lines gets one answer.

People message the way they talk — three fragments in ten seconds. Answering each one separately is the single clearest tell that a business is running a machine.

The detail: a 5-second settle window joins consecutive messages into one question. Photographs queue behind one another per contact, so a caption or a closing word sent while four images are still uploading is still applied to the right case rather than racing it.

Each incoming message is written to your record the moment it arrives, before any of this joining happens — nothing is lost if the reply path fails.

Documents are filed, not dumped.

Open a case with the patient's name, send the images and PDFs as they come out of the machine, close it with one word. The set is read together as one visit rather than as six unrelated files, and the patient identity you declared is the one that wins.

The detail: a file is archived to storage before it is read, and the archived copy is deleted again if the record fails to write. The same forwarded file sent twice is recognised and answered with the item number it already has. There is no half-saved case and no silent duplicate.

If the batch was already closed when a late attachment lands, the attachment is rejected and removed rather than appended to a finished case.

Retrieval by category, not by scrolling.

The reader assigns each document a three-level subject index — region, procedure, stage — plus the kind of study it is, so you can ask for a whole group at once instead of remembering one patient's name.

The detail: the moment you correct an index by hand it is stamped as yours, and later re-index passes will not overwrite it. The machine's guesses are improvable; your corrections are permanent.

Every stored document carries the confidence of its own reading, so a poor photograph is visible as a poor reading rather than passing as fact.

It answers from your documents, not from the air.

Upload your price list, your preparation instructions, your package sheets. Questions are answered from passages retrieved out of those files, with the source material carried into the answer.

The detail: documents are split into roughly 500-word passages that overlap by 50 words, so a sentence that straddles a page break is still findable. Retrieval is scoped to your account at the query itself — not filtered afterwards.

Passages below a relevance floor are discarded rather than padded into the answer, so an unanswerable question is visibly unanswered.

Follow-ups happen days later, without a person remembering.

Write the sequence in plain language once — day one this, day three that. New enquiries are scheduled onto it automatically, and the queue is drained continuously.

The detail: the same message to the same person inside a 24-hour window is cancelled as a duplicate rather than sent twice a second apart — which is what happens when a drip and a bot follow-up both come due on the same tick.

If the chat was handed back to a person, every queued follow-up for that person is cancelled, not merely delayed.

3 · The present arrangement

What you are losing today

  • The knee film you sent yourself at eleven at night — now four hundred photographs down your own gallery, somewhere between a screenshot and a picture of lunch. You know it exists. That is not the same as having it.
  • The Sunday enquiry about a package price, answered on Tuesday, by which time they had already booked down the road and never told you why.
  • The Friday evening a member of staff spent pasting the same paragraph into a hundred and eighty chats, changing one name at a time, and got two of them wrong.
  • The patient who wrote STOP three campaigns ago and is still receiving them, because the list lives in a spreadsheet that never heard him — and because nobody wants to be the person who edits the master sheet.
  • The Monday you discovered the number had been logged out since Friday afternoon, and no one could say which messages had gone and which had not.

None of these are efficiency problems. Each one is a specific patient, on a specific day, who had a worse experience than the one you intended to give them.

6 · Notes for the committee

Separation between accounts

Each account has its own connected number, its own documents, its own contacts and its own automation settings. Ownership is carried on every stored row and applied inside every query rather than as a display filter. Capabilities — clinical records, hospital assistant, staff assistant, voice — are switched on per account, so a practice that only wants outreach sees only outreach.

Where the data lives

The record — patients, documents, conversations, campaigns, outcomes — lives in a database you control, on infrastructure of your choosing. Uploaded clinical files are archived to storage under your own account, addressed by organisation and patient. Nothing about the design requires the record to sit with the vendor.

What a running instance needs

A single application instance with roughly a gigabyte of memory, a managed database, and one storage bucket. The instance is health-checked continuously and the connected numbers survive its restarts, because the pairing is in the database rather than on the instance's disk.

Integration with what you already run

A token-authenticated interface lets an existing laboratory or hospital system hand over report deliveries, bulk sends and status checks without a person opening this application at all — including asking, mid-run, how far a batch has got and stopping it. Enquiries can be pushed in from a spreadsheet or a web form through a per-source secret that can be rotated without touching the source.

7 · On the roadmap

What is not finished, stated before you find it.

A brochure that only lists strengths is a brochure you have to verify line by line. These are the parts we would rather you hear from us.

Voice calling

The control side is complete and in the product: call agents, conversation flows with draft-and-publish, calling campaigns with their own contact lists and pause control, per-call logs, usage accounting, and enrolment of desk or handset devices that lease calls from a queue. What it is not yet is a single-switch install — it runs as a separate service alongside the main application and needs a desk gateway or a paired handset to place the calls.

Status: control plane built · separate deployment required · not part of the standard install

Tappable buttons in outgoing messages

Choices, links and call-back numbers are appended to a message as clearly marked lines — a link line, a call line, a choice line — and replies are read as text. This is deliberate for now because it behaves identically on every handset. Native tappable buttons are not sent. The unsubscribe path is a typed STOP, which is recognised exactly and acted on automatically.

Status: works everywhere · not yet native

Live progress across a restart

Every recipient's outcome — sent, failed, and why — is written to the database as the run proceeds and survives anything. The live counter on screen is held in the running instance, so a restart mid-campaign loses the on-screen tally even though the record and the resume point are intact.

Status: outcomes durable · live counter in memory

Per-account live event channels

The dashboard's live feed — pairing codes, connection state, message activity — is currently published on a single channel to connected dashboard sessions rather than partitioned per account. Stored data is fully separated; this is the live stream only. Scoped channels are the next item on this list.

Status: queued · recommended before multi-tenant rollout

Hospital and staff assistants

Both ship behind per-account switches and are configured at enablement by us rather than self-serve in the interface. Their conversation views, administrator lists and per-person pause controls are complete; the setup is not yet something a practice manager does alone.

Status: functional · assisted setup

Housekeeping

Two superseded service files remain in the codebase alongside their replacements. They are not on any live path and are scheduled for removal at the next consolidation.

Status: inert · removal scheduled
